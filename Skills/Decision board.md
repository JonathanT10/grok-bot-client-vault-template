# Decision board

Tags: #skill #decisions

Five advisors look at a decision on their own, then their views are combined into one recommendation. The board advises; the decider in [[People and roles]] decides.

## When to use
- Decisions that cost real money, take more than a few days, or are hard to undo (a new vendor, a new routine that acts, a price change, hiring help).
- When asked "run it by the board" or "what am I missing?".
- Skip it for small, easy-to-reverse choices; decide and log it.

## Inputs
- The decision as a question, and the options including "do nothing".
- Known facts from the client, the vault, or sourced research. Missing facts are open questions, not guesses.

## The board
- **Operator**: can this get done, by whom, by when? What is the smallest version that works?
- **Skeptic**: what are we assuming, what is the evidence, what would make it fail, is there a simpler option?
- **Customer advocate**: how does this feel to {{CLIENT_NAME}}'s customers and staff? What will annoy them?
- **Finance partner**: up-front and monthly cost, payback, cheapest way to test, what else that money or time could do.
- **Legal/risk advisor**: security, privacy, contract, tax, or safety issues; what data is exposed; is it reversible? Not legal advice; flag when a professional should weigh in.

## Process
1. Start a decision record from the [[Decision record template]] with context and options.
2. Each advisor writes independently: verdict (go / go with changes / no-go), top 2-3 points, one question that must be answered.
3. Synthesis: agreement, disagreement, one-line recommendation, key risks and mitigations, open questions (with who can answer), what would change the call.
4. Stop and give the summary to the decider. Leave Decision and Why blank.
5. When they decide, fill in Decision, Why, Follow-up (with dates), add a log.md line, commit.

## Done when
- Success: the strongest case for and against fits on one screen, open questions are named, and the record shows what was decided and why.
- Failure: five advisors who agree on everything, made-up numbers, or the board deciding for the client.
