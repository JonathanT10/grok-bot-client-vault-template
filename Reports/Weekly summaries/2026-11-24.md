## How each recipe is laid out

Every recipe follows the same shape, so a customer can review them the same way:

| Section | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Status | "Untested recipe", the date its sources were checked, and a reminder to re-check them at setup. |
| Purpose / scope | What the report covers and what it will never do. |
| Configuration | Blanks the client fills: scope, thresholds, lookback, recipient. Times are reported in `{{TIMEZONE}}`. |
| Permissions and approval gate | The read access the report would need. The recipe grants nothing. |
| Read-only workflow | The steps, in plain words. Command names are to be checked against current vendor docs at setup. |
| Suggested cadence | A proposed schedule. It is not saved. |
| Output, evidence, failure and staleness | What the report shows, where its evidence links go, and what to say when data is missing or old. |
| Acceptance tests | Checks against fictional fixtures. They have not been run. |
| Sources checked, Unverified items | The official pages that were read, and every point no source answered. |

## Guardrails to keep

- A recipe is a spec, not a routine. To turn one into a routine, fill its configuration, re-check its sources, and get written approval from `{{PRIMARY_CONTACT}}` first. Use `Templates/Routine spec template.md` for the routine itself.
- Any role assignment a recipe needs is a change to the client's tenant. It needs a written request naming the identity, role, and scope, explicit approval, and an entry in `Reference/Access list.md`. Some read roles allow more than reading. The backup recipe says the report must never use the write permissions that come with its suggested role.
- "No alert" or "no record found" does not mean nothing happened. It means nothing was visible to that identity, in that scope, in that retention window. Reports should say so.
- Remediation, alert rules, export setup, and patch-mode changes are separate approved changes. They are never part of a recipe.
- Standing rules in `README.md` and `GROK.md` still apply. No secrets in the vault. Client data stays in that client's vault. A person approves anything that sends, spends, publishes, deletes, changes permissions, or connects or disconnects an account.

## How to use this week's additions during setup

- During discovery, ask which of the four areas the client actually uses: Azure Backup, Azure VMs, a product list worth watching against KEV, or Jira for help desk work. Delete the recipes that do not apply, and their lines in `index.md`.
- For each recipe you keep, fill the configuration blanks and read its Unverified items list with `{{IT_OWNER}}` or `{{PRIMARY_CONTACT}}`. Do not fill those gaps from memory.
- Start with the KEV watch or the Jira digest if the client wants a quick, low-access first report. The KEV watch reads a public feed. The Jira digest needs read access to the help desk project only.
- Keep the suggested cadence as a proposal until the client approves a schedule.
