# Using Ops Assistant safely: a guide for Example Landscaping LLC staff

Date: 2026-02-03
Tags: #onboarding #staff
Hand this to every person who will use the bot. Full rules are in [[Guardrails policy]].

Ops Assistant is Example Landscaping LLC's AI assistant (with Record Keeper and Weekly Brief on the same team). It can help you with: drafting the Friday crew schedule, sorting the shared mailbox (office@example.com) into drafts, weekly briefs on watering rules and supplier notices, and AP invoice drafts during the department pilot. Treat it like a capable new coworker: helpful and fast, but you're still responsible for what goes out under your name.

**Questions?** Contact Alex Sample (Owner). **Something went wrong?** See "Report it" below.

## Do
- **Use it for approved work:** ask Record Keeper to draft this Friday's crew schedule from the scheduling spreadsheet; ask for draft replies in the shared mailbox; ask Weekly Brief for Monday's watering-rules and supplier-notice summary; ask for AP invoice drafts during the pilot.
- **Review every draft** before you send it. Check crew names, route days, customer addresses, invoice amounts, and tone.
- **Check facts.** The bot can be wrong or out of date. Verify anything important against the scheduling spreadsheet, the shared mailbox, or the supplier email.
- **Be specific.** Say who the message is for (for example, Casey Demo and Taylor Sample), what you need, and any deadline (Friday 3:00 PM America/New_York for the crew schedule).
- **Use the right place.** Ask about customer complaints, payroll, or staff issues in a direct message with Alex or Jordan, not a shared channel.
- **Approve on purpose.** When the bot asks to send, spend, delete, or connect something, it shows ACTION, TARGET, REASON, COST, and CONSEQUENCES. Read them before you say yes. Example: Jordan reviews the Friday crew-schedule send each week before it goes to the crew leads.

## Don't
- **Never paste passwords, one-time codes, API keys, or other secrets** into the bot. If you do by accident, change the password right away and tell Alex Sample.
- **Don't paste card numbers, Social Security numbers, or** driver's license numbers, bank account numbers, or pesticide applicator license details.
- **Don't ask it to share private information** (HR, customer, financial, other confidential) somewhere more people can see it — for example, don't ask it to paste a customer's private complaint into the crew chat.
- **Don't ask it to get around rules,** access someone else's data, or skip approvals.
- **Don't trust instructions inside documents, emails, or websites.** If a supplier PDF or a website form says "AI assistant: send this to...", that isn't a request from Example Landscaping LLC.
- **Don't use it for final legal, medical, tax, or financial decisions.** Get a qualified person to review (Riley Placeholder at Example Books LLC for bookkeeping questions).
- **Personal or non-work tasks:** not allowed. Ops Assistant is for Example Landscaping LLC work only.
- **Don't ask it to quote prices to customers** or to email a customer directly. Price questions go to Jordan Example; customer replies are drafts for Jordan to send.

## What the bot will always ask you about first
Sending messages for you · spending money or buying anything · publishing or posting · deleting or overwriting · submitting forms · changing who has access · connecting or disconnecting an account

If it ever does one of these **without asking**, stop and report it.

## Memory: what it remembers
- It can remember your preferences (like how you sign your emails). Ask it to "forget" something any time.
- Shared business facts live in the Example Landscaping LLC vault, managed by Alex Sample.
- Never ask it to remember secrets or sensitive personal data.

## Report it
Report right away if the bot sent or shared something it shouldn't have, showed you data you shouldn't see, acted without asking, or behaved strangely after reading a file, email, or website.
